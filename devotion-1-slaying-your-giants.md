# Devotion 1: One in a Billion
**Series:** Slaying Your Giants (Kent Crockett), Chapter 1: Hopelessness
**Passage:** Matthew 17:24–27 · **Length:** about 16 minutes (13 plus three true-story slides) · **File:** `firstlight-giants-devotion-1.html`

*Personal-use devotional notes for leading teachers. Not for sale or public distribution. Book content is paraphrased and credited to Kent Crockett.*

**Big idea:** Hopeless situations aren't hopeless to God. He doesn't have to consider the odds. *Without God, one in a billion. With God, one in one.*

**Series pattern (so every devotion feels the same):** Title → Opening thought → Meet the giant → Scripture → 4 teaching slides, with at least one **True story** slide woven in → Big picture (3 steps) → Back to the Bible → Carry these with you → Take it into today. No role plays, no biography slides, no timed silence.

### The three true stories in Devotion 1 (so you can tell them)

**One in 518 million** (slide 3). The author stopped at McDonald's while they were running a Monopoly game with a $1 million prize. The official rules put his chance of winning at one in 518,071,190. He isn't a gambler, but he peeled the sticker off his fries anyway. Not a millionaire, not even a free small drink: he had drawn Atlantic Avenue for the fifth time. That's how our worst situations feel, and it's the number the Big picture slide comes back to.

**God sold the cattle** (Fact 1, slide 7). Soon after Dallas Theological Seminary opened in 1924, it was close to shutting down. The creditors were going to foreclose at noon on a set day. President Lewis Chafer and the faculty were praying in his office, and one of them, Harry Ironside, prayed, "Lord, You own the cattle on a thousand hills. Sell some and send us the money." Meanwhile a Texas businessman walked into the business office. He had just sold two carloads of cattle in Fort Worth and felt he had to give the money away. The secretary took his cheque into the prayer meeting, and it was for the exact amount of the debt. Dr Chafer turned to Dr Ironside and said, "Harry, God sold the cattle!" *Without God, one in a billion. With God, one in one.*

**The last can in the cupboard** (Fact 3, slide 10). David Smith, a young pastor near Farmington, New Mexico, was living on $600 a month with his wife Jackie and their small child. One morning she served him the last can in the cupboard, a bowl of hominy. David marched to his church office and told God exactly what he thought: he'd preached about God's faithfulness for years, so where was it now? After forty-five minutes of ranting, the phone rang. Jackie said, "Come home right now." A woman was carrying groceries to the front door, her station wagon packed to the roof with food. She said she'd felt led to bring it. (The author roomed with David at seminary.)

**Other chapter stories you could add if you want a longer telling:** Jimmy Smith's fishing-tournament fish, where a church deacon's first catch of the day was the $20,000 prize fish (Fact 2), and Franklin Graham landing a plane with dead instruments just as the runway lights came on (Fact 3).

Each story is its own slide, so if you ever want a shorter devotion you can delete one without breaking anything.

# The Recipe: Devotions 2 to 5

## The 12-slide formula (every devotion uses this)

| # | Slide | Min | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | 0.5 | Name the giant and the passage |
| 2 | Opening thought | 1 | One question that makes it personal. No answers needed |
| 3 | Meet the giant | 1 | What this giant is and how it steals the promised land |
| 4 | Scripture | 1.5 | The key verse (KJV). Read the full passage aloud first |
| 5–8 | Four teaching slides | 4 | One idea each, with an "In the classroom" callout |
| + | **True story slide(s)** | 1 each | A real story from the chapter, retold in three short paragraphs, with a pull line and a "why it matters" note. **Every devotion has at least one**, placed next to the teaching slide it supports. |
| 9 | Big picture | 1 | Three labelled steps (number → number → number) that tell the chapter's story |
| 10 | Back to the Bible | 1.5 | Two more verses and three takeaways |
| 11 | Take these with you | 1.5 | Three question cards. No discussion time |
| 12 | Take it into today | 1 | One challenge, a short prayer, and "Tomorrow" |

**Total: about 13 minutes plus 1 minute per true story (so 14 to 15).** The images are the same seven slots every time (`gN-01-title` to `gN-07-closing`), where N is the devotion number.

**Story rule (important):** nothing on the Big picture, Back to the Bible, Take these with you or Take it into today slides may refer to a story, name or example that hasn't already been told earlier in that same devotion. If a later slide needs a story, a True story slide (or at least the full example in a teaching slide) comes first. I check every slide against this before delivering a devotion.

**Tone rules I'm following:** teacher-focused applications, true stories retold in my own words and credited to Kent Crockett (never long quotes), KJV for Scripture, no role plays, no timed silence.

## Devotion 2: Fear
**Book:** Chapter 2 · **Giant:** Fear · **Passage:** Joshua 1:1–9 · **Tomorrow:** Giant 3, Discontentment

**Big idea:** Fear is faith in reverse. It assumes the worst. The answer isn't logic, it's God's presence and trust.

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 Title | *Most fears are* / *just a* / **coatrack** · pills: Giant 2 · Fear / Joshua 1:9 |
| 2 Opening thought | *What are you afraid might go wrong this term?* |
| 3 Meet the giant | Joshua's first day as leader: real enemies, real danger, and God's pep talk, "be strong and courageous." Before we can enter the promise, fear has to be faced. **Today's giant:** Fear, assuming the worst will happen. |
| 4 Scripture | Joshua 1:9 (KJV): "Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest." *Notice: the command comes with a reason.* |
| **Story A: The man in the corner** (before Teaching 1) | Max Wilkins as a boy at his great aunt Ruth's creaky house sees a man standing in the corner of the bedroom. Too scared to run, he lies rigid all night with one eye open. At sunrise it's a coatrack with a hat on it. *Why it matters:* fear is faith in reverse. |
| 5 Teaching 1: **Fear is faith in reverse** | Faith assumes good, fear assumes the worst; both are assumptions about the future. Max's night showed how real a false fear can feel. Most fears are coatracks with hats. Fear can make us hide our gifts (Matthew 25:25). **In the classroom:** the observation, the tricky parent email, the lesson that might flop. |
| **Story: One false trumpet** (after Teaching 1) | 1794, the war on the Rhine. French troops are about to attack an Austrian village; six hundred Austrians are ready, taking their signals from a trumpeter. The French slip their own trumpeter, Joseph Werck, in among them. He sounds "rally", then "retreat". The Austrians flee and the French take the village without a fight. Beaten by a sound, not an enemy. This is what the Big picture slide refers to. |
| 6 Teaching 2: **Believe He is with you** | Joshua 1:9; Psalm 23:4. The disciples panicked in the boat while Jesus slept in it (Mark 4). If He doesn't calm the storm, He will keep you calm in it. **In the classroom:** you are never alone in that room. |
| 7 Teaching 3: **Face what you fear** | The salesman and the "Beware of the dog" signs: it was a puppy on the porch. Fear is a bully that backs down when called out. **In the classroom:** the conversation you've been avoiding. |
| 8 Teaching 4: **Trust, don't just think** | You can't reason your way out of fear, you trust your way out. Psalm 56:3–4. The author's swimming lesson: he floated only when he stopped struggling. **In the classroom:** hand God the things you can't control. |
| **Story B: Lightning and a tent** (after Teaching 4) | The author and two church elders camp by a lake without checking the weather. A violent storm hits, with lightning a few metres from the tent. He remembers the disciples panicking while Jesus slept, and the three men pray, say they trust God, and praise Him. A deep peace comes. The lightning keeps striking, but the fear is gone. |
| 9 Big picture | **1 trumpet**, *How fear works:* the Austrians fled at one false retreat call and lost their village without a fight. → **1 presence**, *What changes it:* "the LORD thy God is with thee." → **0 fears**, *What David found:* "delivered me from all my fears" (Psalm 34:4). |
| 10 Back to the Bible | Psalm 34:4 and Isaiah 41:10 (KJV). Takeaways: **Say it out loud** ("Lord, I believe You are with me"), **Look at it** (face the puppy), **Let go** (trust, like floating). |
| 11 Take these with you | 1. What is your "coatrack," a fear that might just be a hat on a stand? 2. Where do you most need to remember God is with you in the room? 3. What is one fear you could face this week? |
| 12 Close | **Challenge:** Before the thing you dread this week, say Joshua 1:9 quietly to yourself. **Prayer:** about God's presence and courage. **Tomorrow:** Giant 3: Discontentment. |

## Devotion 3: Discontentment
**Book:** Chapter 3 · **Giant:** Discontentment · **Passage:** Philippians 4:10–13 · **Tomorrow:** Giant 4, Doubt

**Big idea:** Contentment isn't found by changing seats; it's a choice made in the heart, right where God has seated you.

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 Title | *May I change* / **seats,** / *please?* · pills: Giant 3 · Discontentment / Philippians 4:11 |
| 2 Opening thought | *If you could swap one "seat" in your life, which would it be?* |
| 3 Meet the giant | A restlessness that says "I'd be happy if only…" Paul wrote about contentment from a Roman prison. **Today's giant:** Discontentment, believing happiness is always one seat, one place or one thing away. |
| 4 Scripture | Philippians 4:11 (KJV): "…for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content." *Notice: he said "learned."* |
| **Story A: May I change seats?** (before Teaching 1) | Preacher Jack Hyles greets the man beside him on a flight from San Francisco to Chicago and gets silence twice. Deciding he's beside a "crab" who will ruin his trip, he asks to change seats. The plane is full, so he stays put and tells the man he's not going to ruin his trip. The man never looks up. |
| 5 Teaching 1: **Accept your assigned seat** | The book's airplane picture: God has assigned you a seat, and you'll probably sit near a "crab." Paul didn't choose prison, yet he learned to be content there. Play the cards you've been dealt (Romans 8:28). **In the classroom:** this class, this room, this timetable. |
| 6 Teaching 2: **Learn to love your crab** | The difficult person God has seated you beside. The grizzly bear lets the skunk eat beside him rather than create a stink. God may be using the crab to teach you to love. **In the classroom:** a tough colleague, parent or student. Choose kindness, not hate. |
| 7 Teaching 3: **Changing seats won't change your heart** | Three myths: *someone else will make me happy*, *somewhere else will*, *one more thing will*. The man who moved his family forty times in ten years forgot to look inside his own heart. The beggar asked for $200 once he got $100: "enough" is never enough. **In the classroom:** the next school, next year group or next role won't fix it alone. *(Note: the book says God may lead us to a new place, just not because discontent drives us.)* |
| **Story B: The job in Wichita** (after Teaching 3) | The author's friend Phil turned down a job in Wichita because something felt wrong, then kicked himself for two years. On a flight he sat beside a man who had taken that very job two years earlier and hated it: constant conflict. Phil realised God had protected him from a mistake. |
| 8 Teaching 4: **Choose joy where you're seated** | Contentment is a decision of the heart. Paul thanked God for the Roman guards who heard the gospel and for the letters that became Scripture. The elderly man waving from his "shack." **In the classroom:** don't let the crab ruin your trip. |
| 9 Big picture | **40 moves**, *The chase:* a family moved forty times in ten years looking for the perfect place. → **1 seat**, *The truth:* God assigned it and Paul learned to be content in his. → **1 choice**, *The answer:* choose to be content wherever you are. |
| 10 Back to the Bible | Romans 8:28 and 1 Timothy 6:6 (KJV). Takeaways: **Thank Him for the seat**, **Pray for your crab**, **Choose it daily**. |
| 11 Take these with you | 1. Which seat in your work do you most wish you could swap? 2. Who might God be using to grow you, and how could you show them kindness this week? 3. What have you been telling yourself "if I just had ___, I'd be happy"? |
| 12 Close | **Challenge:** Each day this week, thank God for one thing about your seat, and do one kind thing for the person who tests you. **Tomorrow:** Giant 4: Doubt. |

2. `g3-02-scripture` (16:9): light streaming through a stone window onto a blank scroll, soft and low-detail.
3. `g3-03-seat` (1:1): a row of aircraft seats from behind, one lit in a mint spotlight.

7. `g3-07-closing` (16:9): a teacher at a desk by a window in morning light, one small plant, calm.

## Devotion 4: Doubt
**Book:** Chapter 4 · **Giant:** Doubt · **Passage:** Matthew 11:2–6 · **Tomorrow:** Giant 5, Temptation

**Big idea:** Even strong believers doubt. Doubt isn't the end of faith; take it to Jesus, hold on to what you know, and trust Him with your heart.

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 Title | *Are you the* / **one,** *or* / *someone else?* · pills: Giant 4 · Doubt / Matthew 11:3 |
| 2 Opening thought | *What question have you been quietly afraid to ask God?* |
| 3 Meet the giant | John the Baptist was bold and certain, until prison. Doubt isn't unbelief, but it isn't faith either; it wavers between the two. **Today's giant:** Doubt, being torn between yes and no. |
| 4 Scripture | Matthew 11:3 (KJV): "Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?" *Notice: John took his doubt straight to Jesus.* Read 11:2–6 aloud first. |
| 5 Teaching 1: **Even the strong doubt** | John, David (Psalm 13:1), Elijah, Jeremiah, even all the disciples. Doubt is being double-minded, tossed like the sea (James 1:6–8). Doubters feel tossed emotionally, pushed around doctrinally and torn mentally. **In the classroom:** "Am I making any difference at all?" |
| 6 Teaching 2: **Question your doubts, not your faith** | John sent his doubts to Jesus, and Jesus answered. John hadn't been wrong, he needed to adjust his understanding (Jesus pointed to His first coming). Doubts can be a stepping stone, not a stumbling block. **In the classroom:** bring the question to God, to Scripture, to a wise colleague. |
| 7 Teaching 3: **Hold on to what you know** | Unanswered questions loom large when we dwell on them. The healed man said, "one thing I do know" (John 9:25). Jesus told John what he could see. Remember that Jesus fulfilled more than 300 prophecies, and recall the times God clearly answered you (tie back to Devotion 1's divine appointments). **In the classroom:** write down three times God came through. |
| 8 Teaching 4: **Trust with your heart, not just your head** | Proverbs 3:5. Doubt always has "reasonable" reasons. A fighter pilot in the dark felt he was climbing, but his instruments said he was diving; he trusted the instruments and avoided the mountains. God's Word and Spirit are our instruments. **In the classroom:** when your feelings say "it's not working," what does God's Word say? |
| **Story: Trust the instruments** (after Teaching 4) | Col. Jeff Patton, a college friend of the author, flew an F-15 on the first night of the Gulf War. Enemy radar locked on, so he threw his jet around to break the lock, which left him disoriented. His body insisted he was climbing; his instruments said he was in a 60-degree dive. He trusted the instruments, pulled up hard, and levelled out about 2,000 feet above the mountains. |
| 9 Big picture | **2 minds**, *How doubt feels:* double-minded, pulled both ways. → **300+ prophecies**, *What we know:* Jesus fulfilled more than 300. → **1 mind**, *Where trust leads:* one settled mind that follows God's instruments. |
| 10 Back to the Bible | James 1:6–8, Proverbs 3:5, Philippians 4:8 (KJV). Takeaways: **Take it to Jesus**, **Count what you know**, **Follow the instruments**. |
| 11 Take these with you | 1. What doubt (about God, your calling or your work) have you been carrying quietly? 2. What is one thing you know for certain God has done? 3. Where are your feelings saying one thing and God's Word another? |
| 12 Close | **Challenge:** Write down one doubt and one thing you know is true, and put them side by side. **Tomorrow:** Giant 5: Temptation. |

**Image concepts:**
1. `g4-01-title` (16:9): a lone silhouette at a barred stone window looking out at a distant road lit in mint. Left side empty.
2. `g4-02-scripture` (16:9): a long road splitting into two paths in dawn mist, soft and low-detail.
3. `g4-03-tossed` (1:1): a huge wave rising with mint spray against an indigo sky.
4. `g4-04-question` (1:1): a hand releasing a folded blank letter toward a beam of light.
5. `g4-05-known` (1:1): a single lantern in a dark room casting a clear circle of mint light.

7. `g4-07-closing` (16:9): a teacher standing at a fork in a path at sunrise, with one clear path of mint light.

## Devotion 5: Temptation
**Book:** Chapter 5 · **Giant:** Temptation · **Passage:** Genesis 3:1–6 · **Tomorrow:** Giant 6, Unforgiveness

**Big idea:** Adam and Eve's story is our instruction manual: avoid the place, run instead of resisting, and be filled with what God provides so you aren't hungry for what He forbids.

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 Title | *Adam and Eve's* / **instruction** / *manual* · pills: Giant 5 · Temptation / 1 Corinthians 10:13 |
| 2 Opening thought | *Where do you keep ending up, even after telling yourself you wouldn't?* |
| 3 Meet the giant | If Adam and Eve left an instruction manual on temptation, what would it say? The tactics haven't changed; only the bait has. **Today's giant:** Temptation, trying to meet a God-given need in the wrong way. |
| 4 Scripture | 1 Corinthians 10:13 (KJV): "…God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it." *Notice: God provides the exit.* Read Genesis 3:1–6 aloud first. |
| 5 Teaching 1: **Avoid the place** | God put the tree in the middle of the garden so they would know where *not* to go. Temptation starts with a thought to go there; the door opens when we decide we want to be tempted (Romans 13:14); Satan waits at the place. "Lead us not into temptation." **In the classroom:** your own "middle of the garden": the staffroom complaining, the late-night scroll, the habit you know trips you. |
| 6 Teaching 2: **It blinds you and pulls harder as you near it** | Once inside, we stop seeing consequences ("it looks good for food"). The nearer we get, the weaker our willpower. The chauffeur who stays as far from the cliff as possible got the job. Cut it off at the first thought. **In the classroom:** stop it before it becomes a plan. |
| 7 Teaching 3: **Run, don't just resist** | "I'm trying not to!" The Bible never tells us to try harder to resist temptation; it tells us to flee (2 Timothy 2:22; Joseph, Genesis 39:12). "Let's run and pray!" **In the classroom:** leave the room, close the app, change the subject. |
| 8 Teaching 4: **Eat the fruit God provided** | Temptation is a God-given need met the wrong way. Full from Grandma's turkey dinner, the apple pie no longer tempts.
