# Devotion 10: Don't Trust the Carnival Mirror
**Series:** Slaying Your Giants (Kent Crockett), Chapter 10: Self-Image
**Passage:** Psalm 139:13–18 (read aloud first; key verse 139:14) · **Length:** about 15 minutes (13 plus two true-story slides) · **Files:** `index.html` + `data/devotion-10.js`

*Personal-use devotional notes for leading teachers. Not for sale or public distribution. Book content is paraphrased and credited to Kent Crockett.*

**Big idea:** Self-image is a self-portrait, and most of us paint it from warped mirrors: looks, brains, abilities and success. Love the person God made, lose the selfishness, and stop competing so you can help your neighbour. *Fearfully and wonderfully made.*

**Series pattern:** Title → Opening thought → Meet the giant → Scripture → 4 teaching slides, with at least one **True story** slide woven in → Big picture (3 steps) → Back to the Bible → Carry these with you → Take it into today. No role plays, no biography slides, no timed silence.

### The two true stories (so you can tell them)

**The verse that didn't seem to fit** (slide 8). Christian psychologist Dr James Michaelson was counselling a woman who felt lonely and abandoned. While she talked, one verse kept running through his mind, and it seemed to have nothing to do with her problem: "It is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves" (Psalm 100:3). When she finished, he said it, though he feared it would sound foolish. She broke down. She had been born before her parents were married, and all her life she had believed she was a mistake that God hadn't made. When he spoke the verse she pictured God forming her in her mother's womb. "Now I know that God created me, and I'm not a mistake. I'll never be the same again." (The author was told this by Dr Michaelson himself.)

**The home run she couldn't finish** (slide 10). In 2008, Western Oregon played Central Washington in college softball with a place in the NCAA Division II playoffs on the line. Central Washington led 2–1. Sara Tucholsky, one of Western Oregon's weakest hitters (batting .153, never a home run), hit one over the centre-field fence. At first base she tore a knee ligament and collapsed. The rules said her teammates couldn't touch her or she'd be out. Central Washington's first baseman, Mallory Holtman, asked the umpire if the rules said anything about the other team. They didn't. So Holtman and a teammate carried Sara around the bases, pausing so she could touch each one with her good leg. Western Oregon won 4–2, and Central Washington missed the playoffs.

**Other stories in the chapter if you want a longer telling:** the prison chaplain who was once a convict (Step 1 context), and the psychiatrist joke ("the bad news is you really are inferior").

### Story-rule check
Nothing on the Big picture, Back to the Bible, Carry these with you or Take it into today slides refers to a story that hasn't been told earlier. The twelve actors and the four mirrors (slide 5), the counselling office (8) and the softball game (10) all come first. "Hang a new portrait" on the closing slide refers to the self-portrait image introduced on slide 3. The Big picture's "carried a stranger around the bases" refers to slide 10.

### Pastoral notes (please check)
- **Body image and suicide.** The chapter notes some people have considered suicide over how they look. I left that line out of the slides. If you think someone in your group may be carrying heavy self-hatred, you might add a quiet line that they can speak to you or a counsellor afterwards.
- **Kamikaze funeral illustration left out.** The book illustrates "a funeral for selfishness" with the WWII suicide pilots who attended their own funerals. I used the Luke 9:23 "deny yourself, take up your cross daily" idea alone, which is gentler for a staffroom.
- **Prison chaplain story left out.** The claim that prisoners offend because they hate themselves is a broad generalisation, and the story is long. It's in "other stories" if you want it.
- **Dated statistics left out.** The Louis Harris poll (1987) and the valedictorian study are dropped. The Hollywood actor survey stays but is described as "a survey the author cites," since the source is old (1974).
- **"Hate" idiom.** I explain Luke 14:26 simply: "hate" means prefer Him above everyone. Check you're comfortable with that wording.
- **The Michaelson story** mentions the woman was born before her parents married. I kept it gentle ("born before her parents were married"), but cut the detail if it would be too close to home for anyone in your group.
- **Spiritual warfare softened.** The book says the devil wants you to see yourself as worthless. I left that out and kept the focus on God's word about you, in keeping with Devotion 9.
- **Passage choice.** The chapter itself leans on Psalm 139:14, Matthew 22:39 and Philippians 2:4. I read Psalm 139:13–18 aloud with 139:14 as the key verse; 2 Samuel 9:8 ("dead dog") from the chapter epigraph is not used.
- **Day numbering** follows Devotions 6 to 9 (Week 2, Day 10). Change on the title slide if your weeks are laid out differently.
