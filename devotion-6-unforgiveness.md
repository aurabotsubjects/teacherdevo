# Devotion 6: The Debt You Can't Collect
**Series:** Slaying Your Giants (Kent Crockett), Chapter 6: Unforgiveness
**Passage:** Matthew 18:21–35 · **Length:** about 16 minutes (13 plus three true-story slides) · **Files:** `index.html` + `data/devotion-6.js` + `data/devotion-6-images.js`

*Personal-use devotional notes for leading teachers. Not for sale or public distribution. Book content is paraphrased and credited to Kent Crockett.*

**Big idea:** Unforgiveness is a debt we keep trying to collect, and it locks us up, not them. We forgive because we have been forgiven far more. *Forgiven much, forgave little.*

**Series pattern:** Title → Opening thought → Meet the giant → Scripture → 4 teaching slides, with at least one **True story** slide woven in → Big picture (3 steps) → Back to the Bible → Carry these with you → Take it into today. No role plays, no biography slides, no timed silence.

**Life stories in this devotion (3):** the parable Jesus told (slide 6), the author's own story (slide 8) and the Revolutionary War pastor (slide 11). Stories alternate with teaching slides so no two stories sit back to back.

### The three true stories (so you can tell them)

**The servant who was forgiven** (slide 6, Matthew 18:23–35). A servant owed his king a debt so huge he could never repay it. He fell on his knees and begged for time, and the king cancelled the whole thing. The servant walked out, found a fellow servant who owed him about a hundred days' wages, grabbed him by the throat and demanded payment. The man begged for time with the very same words, and he had him thrown into prison anyway. When the king heard, he called him wicked and handed him over to the jailers. (Retold from Jesus' parable. The book puts the first debt at about $50 million and the second at about $15, which is a fun way to show the size gap.)

**I thought I had forgiven him** (slide 8). A friend of the author's turned on him for no clear reason and began slandering him. Kent phoned to ask what was wrong and offered to talk, and the man yelled "No!" and hung up. Two years later, in a shop, the man chatted as if nothing had happened. Kent kept his eyes down and said very little. His wife said afterwards, "You hardly even looked at him." He realised he hadn't truly forgiven him, so he watched the man walk away and prayed, "Lord, I choose to forgive him." Compassion came instantly and the hurt was gone. The man never apologised, and it no longer mattered. (Told in the book, ch. 6.)

**Sixty miles to save an enemy** (slide 11). During the American Revolutionary War, a pastor named John Miller heard that one of his enemies was to be hanged. He walked sixty miles to ask General Washington to spare the man. Washington said he was sorry but he couldn't pardon Miller's friend. Miller said, "He's not my friend. He is my worst enemy." Seeing that, Washington signed the pardon. Miller carried it another fifteen miles and arrived just as the man was walking toward the gallows. The enemy went free, set free by the pastor he had hated and persecuted. (Told in the book, from Carl Windsor's *On This Day*.)

**Other stories in the chapter if you want a longer telling:**
- The boy on the park bench who sat on a bumblebee: "Why don't you get up?" "Because I figure I'm hurting him more than he's hurting me." Short and funny, it fits Teaching 3.
- The author at a Bible conference, where a speaker's message on releasing people made him forgive two men. Within a week one came to his door in tears asking forgiveness, and the other walked into his church. It fits Teaching 4.

### Story-rule check
Nothing on the Big picture, Back to the Bible, Take these with you or Take it into today slides refers to a story or example that hasn't been told earlier. The two debts (slide 6) come before they are compared (slide 7) and counted on the Big picture (slide 12). The decision to say "I cancel this debt" (slide 5) comes before the author's prayer (slide 8). "Seventy times seven" is on the scripture slide (4) before it appears again on slide 12.

### Pastoral notes (please check)
- **Miller story tradition.** The book names the pastor John Miller. Many other tellings call him Peter Miller, and the details vary between versions, so the slide is tagged "A Revolutionary War story" rather than "A true story." You could introduce it as "a story that's often told."
- **Real-life hurt.** Some in the room may be thinking of serious harm, abuse or someone still unsafe. The Hand it over slide already says forgiving doesn't mean trusting again at once or pretending it didn't hurt. You may want to say that out loud.
- **Left out of the book.** The book's graphic details of Nineveh's cruelty and its comparison to Hitler, plus the extended "dungeon with a whip" picture, are not on the slides. The book's six keys are condensed into the four teaching slides.
- **The parable's size gap.** Slides say "10,000 talents" and "100 days' wages" and leave out the dollar figures, which are the author's estimates.
