# Devotion 8: Step Out of the Boat
**Series:** Slaying Your Giants (Kent Crockett), Chapter 8: Change
**Passage:** Matthew 14:25–29 (read 14:22–33 aloud) · **Length:** about 15 minutes (13 plus two true-story slides) · **Files:** `index.html` + `data/devotion-8.js`

*Personal-use devotional notes for leading teachers. Not for sale or public distribution. Book content is paraphrased and credited to Kent Crockett.*

**Big idea:** God places many of His blessings outside our comfort zones and asks us to come and get them. Step over the rim on His word, keep your eyes on Him, and He'll catch you. *Here is God.*

**Series pattern:** Title → Opening thought → Meet the giant → Scripture → 4 teaching slides, with at least one **True story** slide woven in → Big picture (3 steps) → Back to the Bible → Carry these with you → Take it into today. No role plays, no biography slides, no timed silence.

### The two true stories (so you can tell them)

**The map with dragons on it** (slide 7). In 1525 an unknown mapmaker drew a chart of the east coast of North America, which is now in the British Museum. Across the unexplored land and sea he wrote "Here be giants," "Here be fiery scorpions" and "Here be dragons." The chart later came into the hands of the scientist Sir John Franklin, who scratched out the warnings and wrote "Here is God" across the unknown. Fear fills the unexplored with dragons, and faith says God is already there. Peter could step onto uncharted water for the same reason. (Told in the book, from Walter Knight's *Master Book of New Illustrations*.)

**The man who couldn't walk across a stage** (slide 9). The author had terrible stage fright. In second grade he was too embarrassed to ask to go to the toilet and it ended the way you'd expect. In fifth grade he sweated for weeks over a Christmas play whose only job was walking across the stage. As a senior his hands shook so much he could barely read the class poem. Then in his last year of college God called him to preach. Minutes before speaking at a church in Olney, Texas, he cried out that God would have to change him right then. He sensed God say: stop thinking about what the congregation thinks of you, and think about what they need to hear. The shaking stopped. He had a purpose, and it gave him courage.

**Other stories in the chapter if you want a longer telling:** the Persian general and the big black door (Teaching 2), Babe Ruth's 1,330 strikeouts and Michael Jordan being cut from his team (Teaching 2), and the Bill Murray "baby steps" film scene (Teaching 3).

### Story-rule check
Nothing on the Big picture, Back to the Bible, Take these with you or Take it into today slides refers to a story or example that hasn't been told earlier. The rim (slide 5), both feet (8) and the hand that caught Peter (10) all come first, as do the baloney and the dragons.

### Pastoral notes (please check)
- **Persian general story left out.** It's a parable-style illustration rather than a documented event, so I put the "big black door" image into Teaching 2 in one sentence and gave the slot to the dated map story.
- **Stage-fright story keeps the second-grade toilet detail,** told in one clean line. It's a fair laugh for primary teachers. Cut it if you'd like.
- **Edison** is in Teaching 2 as a one-line example. The sports examples (Ruth, Jordan) and the Bill Murray scene are out, to keep the slide short.
- **Day numbering** follows Devotion 6 and 7 (Week 2, Day 8). Change on the title slide if your weeks are laid out differently.
