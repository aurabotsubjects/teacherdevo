# Devotion 9: Stop the Movie in Your Mind
**Series:** Slaying Your Giants (Kent Crockett), Chapter 9: Worry
**Passage:** Matthew 6:25–34 (read aloud first; key verse 6:34) · **Length:** about 15 minutes (13 plus two true-story slides) · **Files:** `index.html` + `data/devotion-9.js`

*Personal-use devotional notes for leading teachers. Not for sale or public distribution. Book content is paraphrased and credited to Kent Crockett.*

**Big idea:** Worry runs disaster movies in the theatre of your mind and asks you to believe them. Stop the movie, pray and let go, trust the pilot, and take one day at a time. *Sufficient unto the day.*

**Series pattern:** Title → Opening thought → Meet the giant → Scripture → 4 teaching slides, with at least one **True story** slide woven in → Big picture (3 steps) → Back to the Bible → Carry these with you → Take it into today. No role plays, no biography slides, no timed silence.

### The two true stories (so you can tell them)

**The caller who wasn't a doctor** (slide 6). Before mobile phones, the wife of a Kansas delivery driver the author knew took a call while her husband was on the road. A professional-sounding man said he was a doctor: her husband was in hospital with a life-threatening, unknown disease and she might have it too. He told her to cut off all her hair at the roots for a lab sample. She did. When she asked what to do next, he said to wait for her husband to come home, because he had made up the whole story. The point: she acted on what she believed, not on what was true, and worry works the same way.

**The boy who quit worrying** (slide 8). The author's son Scott moved from a small private school to a large middle school in seventh grade and complained about every adjustment. His dad prayed with him nightly and told him not to worry; Scott said it wasn't that easy. By November he'd stopped complaining. When asked, he said he'd quit worrying about it, because "worrying was harder on me than actually going through it."

**Other stories in the chapter if you want a longer telling:** the woman who diagnosed herself from a medical book (Teaching 1), and the Kansas highway drive where God said "Enjoy the moment" (already one sentence in Teaching 4).

### Story-rule check
Nothing on the Big picture, Back to the Bible, Carry these with you or Take it into today slides refers to a story that hasn't been told earlier. The phone call (slide 6), the toothpicks (10), and the pilot (9) all come first. "Hand over the controls" in the Back slide and the prayer refers to the pilot illustration in slide 9.

### Pastoral notes (please check)
- **Violent-movie openers left out.** The chapter opens with three real incidents of people copying films (two murders and a cruise-ship drowning). I left them out for a teachers' devotion and began with the movie-in-your-mind image instead.
- **Spiritual warfare softened.** The book says many worrying thoughts are planted by the devil. I kept the authority idea (2 Corinthians 10:5, James 4:7) but phrased it as "whether it's your own habit or an enemy's whisper" so it works for every teacher. Add the book's stronger wording if your group is comfortable with it.
- **The hair-cutting story** is told in three short paragraphs. It's a mild laugh, but you can cut the detail if you'd like.
- **Medical-book joke and Titanic/film incidents** are out; the 90 percent joke stays as the light moment in Teaching 2.
- **Passage choice.** The chapter itself leans on Matthew 6:31–34 and Philippians 4:6. I read Matthew 6:25–34 aloud, with 6:34 as the key verse.
- **Day numbering** follows Devotions 6 to 8 (Week 2, Day 9). Change on the title slide if your weeks are laid out differently.
