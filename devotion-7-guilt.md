# Devotion 7: Cancel Your Guilt Trips
**Series:** Slaying Your Giants (Kent Crockett), Chapter 7: Guilt
**Passage:** Matthew 27:20–25 · **Length:** about 15 minutes (13 plus two true-story slides) · **Files:** `index.html` + `data/devotion-7.js`

*Personal-use devotional notes for leading teachers. Not for sale or public distribution. Book content is paraphrased and credited to Kent Crockett.*

**Big idea:** Guilt isn't removed by scrubbing, blaming or hiding. It's removed by being honest with God and receiving what Jesus already paid for. *Washing your hands never cleans your heart.*

**Series pattern:** Title → Opening thought → Meet the giant → Scripture → 4 teaching slides, with at least one **True story** slide woven in → Big picture (3 steps) → Back to the Bible → Carry these with you → Take it into today. No role plays, no biography slides, no timed silence.

### The two true stories (so you can tell them)

**The honest man in the prison** (slide 8). A prince visiting the naval prison at Toulon in France was allowed to free one condemned man. He went from cell to cell asking why each man was there, and every one said he'd been falsely accused. Then one man said, "I'm guilty, and I deserve to be here." The prince called out loudly that such a wretch shouldn't be left among so many "honest" men, then told the officer, "This is the man. Release him at once." The ones who defended themselves stayed behind bars. The one who told the truth walked out free. (Told in the book; originally from *Our Daily Bread*.)

**Remember the duck** (slide 10). A boy visiting his grandparents was given his first slingshot. On impulse he aimed at Grandma's pet duck and the stone hit. In a panic he hid it in the woodpile, but his sister Sally had seen. She said nothing until chores came around, then announced, "Johnny wants to do the dishes," and whispered, "Remember the duck." He did her dishes and her jobs while she went fishing with Grandpa. After several days he confessed. Grandma hugged him. She had watched it from the window and forgiven him long ago. She'd only wondered how long he'd let Sally make a slave of him. (Told in the book, adapted from Richard Hoefler's *Will Daylight Come?*)

**Other stories in the chapter if you want a longer telling:** the San Diego courtroom, where two robbery suspects raised their hands before a witness could answer (Teaching 1); the woman on the radio who asked forgiveness daily for seventeen years (Teaching 3); the coconut cream pie cover-up (Teaching 2).

### Story-rule check
Nothing on the Big picture, Back to the Bible, Take these with you or Take it into today slides refers to a story or example that hasn't been told earlier in the devotion. Pilate's bowl (slides 4–5), Peter and Judas (6), the two steps (7 and 9) and the duck (10) all come first.

### Pastoral notes (please check)
- **Method 4 left out.** The chapter's fourth "wrong method" is "get professional help," and the book is quite dismissive of counselling. For a room of teachers (some of whom may be seeing a counsellor, or should be) I left it out rather than risk it landing badly.
- **Sensitive details kept general.** The chapter mentions Judas's suicide, an abortion call-in and a fatal teenage truck accident. I've said none of these directly. The friend's story is told as "a teenage accident" only, and I haven't named the woman's caller story at all. Tell me if you'd like any of them back.
- **Free-from-guilt line.** "It felt as if it had never happened" is on Teaching 4, from the author's friend, told in a single sentence so it doesn't need its own slide.
- **Blood-of-Jesus language** (Matthew 27:25, 1 John 1:7) is stated plainly on slide 7. Soften if your audience varies.
